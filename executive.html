September 2026 Digital Marketing: Executive Summary

Zayed National Museum · Website · Paid Media · Influencers · Social · Email

For leadership | No targets are set yet, so results are compared with benchmarks and between channels | Every headline figure carries a confidence mark

September at a Glance

The museum welcomed 71,476 visitors in September. Paid campaigns more than doubled website traffic, and 2,566 online ticket bookings were recorded on $139.4K of paid media. Search and Meta drive bookings; LinkedIn and X don't. Influencer reach was large but came from one event and two creators. The biggest risk is measurement: half of bookings lose their source, so we can't yet say reliably which spend produces them.

What a Booking Costs by Channel

The ad platforms and the website disagree on how many bookings each channel made, so cost per booking is shown both ways. Search and Meta are cheapest either way. X costs about 3 to 8 times as much, depending on the view, and LinkedIn produced 2 GA4 bookings for $9.95K.

Platform view: spend ÷ purchases the platform reports. GA4 view: spend ÷ booking sessions GA4 credits to that platform (last click). Platforms over-claim; GA4 under-credits because half of bookings lose their source. The true figure is likely between the two. LinkedIn ($4,976 per GA4 booking, 0 platform purchases) is left off the scale. Epsilon display ($18.0K) has no tracked bookings.

Six Things Leadership Should Know

1. Traffic doubled; quality fell

Users rose 133% (deck), with 69% from paid. Paid social sessions last about 30 seconds and book at under 1%, against 1.17% for Google Ads.

Judge paid social on bookings, not sessions · Paid media
2. Search and Meta earn their budget

Together they took $90.8K (65% of spend) and made 1,081 GA4 bookings. LinkedIn and X took $19.9K for 43.

Move LinkedIn and X budget · Paid media
3. Measurement hides what works

49% of bookings lose their source. Platforms claim 2,545 purchases, 99% of all online bookings. Website sessions from influencers can't be identified.

Fix booking tracking and UTMs · Web & analytics
4. Influencer reach rests on one event

The Creators Immersion visit drove 51M impressions. Two creators made about half. Only 4.2% of creators' followers are in the UAE, against 88% of website users.

Repeat the event; add GCC creators · Influencer agency
5. Members aren't opening emails

Member-only emails were opened by 2.8–3.5% of recipients, against 37.9% for the same campaign sent to the full list. That suggests a delivery or list problem.

Investigate urgently · CRM
6. Organic social is healthy

6,290 new followers. Without paid TikTok, engagement is 2.39%, inside the 1–5% benchmark; the 0.18% headline is a TikTok effect.

Swap carousels for short video · Social

Visitor Experience

Visitors rate the museum highly. September's 169 Google reviews averaged 4.80 out of 5, with 86% at five stars, in line with the 94% satisfaction in the museum's own surveys. Reviewers praise Sheikh Zayed's story, the building and the staff. Only 5 reviews (3%) gave 3 stars or fewer.

What visitors praise

Sheikh Zayed's story and UAE history (47% of written reviews), the falcon-inspired building (29%), and the staff and exhibits (27% each).

What to fix

The one 1-star review with text was about security staff telling a father not to carry his child on his shoulders. That's a rule to explain better, not an exhibit problem.

What to watch

Reviews doubled in the second half of September (54 to 115). The website (4 responses) and retail (2) surveys are too small to report.

Notes for the Report Team

  • Placeholder text. The master deck's summary slide still contains "64M" placeholder text seven times in the file. It may be hidden behind other elements on screen, but should be removed.
  • Wrong month. The app slide is titled "August".
  • Website visits vs users. The "Website visits" tile shows website users (175.5K); website sessions were 219.3K. Use "website sessions" for online visits and keep "visits" for museum visits (71,476), so the two aren't confused.
  • Influencer impressions. The summary shows 2M (Back to School only) and leaves out Always-On's 51.03M. All 4 Back to School influencers are also among Always-On's 49, along with about 46 of their posts (51 of the 62 in the agency's monitor), so the two can't be added.
  • "All metrics grew". The website slide reports engagement down 6.8% and session time down 36%.
  • Tiny survey samples. Website (4 responses) and retail (2) satisfaction shouldn't be shown as percentages.
  • Booking source. Name the system behind "online ticket bookings" on the slide, and say whether it counts orders or tickets.
  • Google reviews. The social deck counts 33 Google Reviews in its community figures; there were 169 in September (70 with text).
  • Lead with visits. Museum visits (71,476 in September, DCT Sites visitor dashboard) are the main outcome and aren't in the current deck. The dashboard's month filter would also give August and year-to-date figures.
